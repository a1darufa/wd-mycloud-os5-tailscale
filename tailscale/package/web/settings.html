    Settings

    Leave empty to use the NAS's own name. Other devices find the NAS under this name; its tailnet addresses stay the same. One range per line, such as your home network, so devices on your tailnet can reach other devices there through this NAS. Approve the routes in the admin console as well. Devices on your tailnet can then send their internet traffic (IPv4) through the NAS's connection. Approve it in the admin console as well. Tailscale replaces its own program when a new version comes out.
    Changes apply at once, without a restart. Routes and the exit node turn on IP forwarding on the NAS, and off again when you remove them. An IPv6 route also turns on IPv6 forwarding, which stops the NAS from taking its own IPv6 address from your router.

    This NAS's kernel can't translate addresses (it has no NAT), so traffic forwarded for routes or the exit node keeps its tailnet address. It only works if your router sends replies for 100.64.0.0/10 back to the NAS.

    Disconnect, log out or switch tailnet

    You opened this page through Tailscale. Each of these cuts that connection at once, and this page stops answering.

    Think twice if the NAS is far away: Tailscale may be the only way to reach it.

    • Disconnect takes the NAS offline but keeps it logged in; Connect brings it back.
    • Log out leaves this tailnet. Logging in again makes the NAS a new device with a new address.
    • Connect to another tailnet logs in to a different account or tailnet. This tailnet's login stays on the NAS, and you can come back to it.

    Not on this page, on purpose

    Tailscale SSH, and using other devices' routes or exit nodes, can each cut this NAS off your tailnet in ways that are hard to undo from here. If you need them, use the tailscale command over SSH. Switching Run App OFF, or uninstalling the app, also disconnects the NAS.

    Where things are

    • Identity and settings: /mnt/HD/HD_a2/.systemfile/tailscale/state (kept on uninstall)
    • Logs: /mnt/HD/HD_a2/.systemfile/tailscale/log
    • Command line, over SSH: /mnt/HD/HD_a2/Nas_Prog/tailscale/bin/tailscale
    • Alerts: if the NAS gets logged out, its login is about to expire, or a version doesn't start, the dashboard's bell icon says so, and so does e-mail if alert e-mails are set up (Settings, Notifications). WD titles these "App Update Available"; the text after WD's own sentence starts with "Tailscale:".

    After installing a new version, the dashboard may hang on "Updating"; refresh it. Tailscale keeps running throughout, and puts Run App back the way it was. If the dashboard still shows OFF, switch it ON yourself.
